# Apollo.RGA Android — visual direction contract

Mode: Operate. Scope: Android onboarding, subscription home and ordinary host selection. The approved Apollo Orbit identity is fixed; this brief does not change the product or authorize release.

## Mechanism and scene

One existing Remnawave subscription presents a small set of ordinary exits. The user needs to see whether access is usable, connect once, and pick the destination country while understanding that the path crosses RU Relay. The typical scene is a phone on unstable Wi-Fi/LTE, often returned to after sleep. First screen must prove state and next action in seconds, not expose a transport control panel.

The rut is a glowing VPN speedometer and a wall of identical neon server cards; its predictable opposite is a sterile settings list. Neither communicates the subscription state and destination faithfully.

## Grounded worlds considered

1. Airline wayfinding: destination hierarchy and recognisable country cues, but airport urgency would overstate errors.
2. Boarding pass: one route and one action, but poor fit for multiple hosts and ongoing connection state.
3. Metro interchange map: relay and exit paths are clear, but the graph would overload a small phone.
4. Nautical chart: explicit path and safe fallback, but too much notation for everyday connection.
5. Apollo mission atlas: an orbital route line, stable destination list, crisp status vocabulary and calm dark-blue field. This is the committed world, translated through the user-approved Apollo Orbit mark rather than a literal flight simulator.
6. Passport index: great country recognition, weak live state and control.
7. Swiss public signage: strong scanning and accessibility, too neutral on its own for approved Apollo identity.

## Committed direction

The first viewport leads with Apollo Orbit at a compact human scale, subscription status in plain language, and one prominent connect/disconnect action. A single route line under the active destination indicates “устройство → RU Relay → страна выхода” without making the relay flag the host icon. Ordinary hosts sit below as clear rows, not equal-size nested cards. Destination flag and country lead; protocol and relay are secondary; each row has a concise purpose. The selected row is stable across refresh. Empty, loading, limited, expired, refresh-failed and connected states have distinct wording and one recovery action.

Visual system: #07182F navy ground from the approved mark, white primary information, #33E6FF→#3187FF→#9B5CFF reserved for the route/primary action, restrained semantic warning/error. Native Material 3 hit areas, focus/back/insets, contrast and dynamic font size govern every screen. Typography is clean, not a faux terminal. Motion is one short route activation when connection is established; no constant orbit animation or false “connected” while dialing.

Cross-surface reach: onboarding introduces the same route vocabulary, host list shares the same status and selection semantics, future restricted-network mode can be a separate named area above ordinary hosts only after admission. The pinned brand appears consistently in launcher and UI. Honest risk: orbital imagery can look like technical telemetry; keep it subordinate to status, action and destination text.

## Concept-seed challenger rulings

The assigned grounded direction was #5. The fetched challengers did not beat it on both audience recognition and product clarity:

- Film cutting bench — declined; kept discipline: resume at the selected frame/host after return.
- Normalling jackfield — declined; kept discipline: distinguish connected, held and broken path without relying on color alone.
- Four-shade handheld console — declined; kept discipline: state and active destination readable at small size.
- Festival lineup typography — declined; kept discipline: one unambiguous main action with strong typographic rank.
- Gravity-rain garden — declined; kept discipline: motion follows a meaningful destination, never decoration.
- Tensegrity column — declined; kept discipline: a failed link has an explicit failure state instead of collapsing the whole composition.

The literal styling of those challengers is not carried into Apollo.RGA. User-approved Apollo Orbit and the subscription UX are binding; no extra approval round is needed for this code-led Android implementation.
